## Vox / Box Paper Collage  (`vox`)

```text
Vox / Box Paper Collage — editorial paper-collage explainer engine (rebuilt from reference frames).

IDENTITY
  A premium editorial explainer built like a hand-made magazine spread that moves. Black-and-white halftone
  cut-outs of people, places and objects are layered on real paper with ONE spot colour per scene, giant
  numerals behind the subject, highlighter headlines and pen annotations. Smart, tactile, data-literate.

REFERENCES THIS ENGINE IS BUILT AROUND
  * "1925" / "1927"   a GIANT numeral in spot colour (mustard / navy) sits BEHIND halftone black-and-white
                      cut-outs (a walking woman, Statue of Liberty, a ship, a desk lamp with a light cone)
                      with tiny paper annotation labels left and right of it
  * "Porsche 356"     yellow highlighter STRIP with a bold serif headline, a small serif sub-line
                      ("The price at launch is $3,750") and ONE clean cut-out car on graph paper
  * "Higgs + Claude"  strip headline across the top, a person on a yellow CIRCLE, torn-paper cut-outs
                      (film camera, reel, TV) pinned around, hand-drawn dashed arrows, small icon chips
  * hands collage     B&W hands + ONE yellow object (glove, notebook) on torn paper, black pen LOOPS
                      and scattered ransom-note letters tying everything together
  * bar chart         serif title, thin source line, textured horizontal bars on graph paper
  * Capitol           halftone building with orange colour BLOCKS and a glitch-pixel edge

WHAT MAKES IT PROFESSIONAL (and why the old version looked flat)
  1. Cut-outs are HALFTONE B&W prints with one spot colour -- never a coloured picture in a frame
  2. Depth: giant type and colour blocks sit BEHIND the subject (layer order), soft ground shadow beneath it
  3. Every edge is hand-made: torn paper with a white fibre rim, scissor-cut border, tiny stop-motion "boil"
  4. The surface is real: crumpled white paper / graph paper / kraft, with a vignette -- never a flat fill
  5. Type is editorial: Playfair + Noto Serif Devanagari, ink-texture (pinholes), highlighter strips
  6. Annotations are paper labels and hand-drawn arrows/loops, never vector pills
  7. Colour discipline: ink black + ONE spot colour per scene (mustard OR navy OR orange); the rest is paper

ITEM TYPES TO USE
  bigtext      giant numeral/word behind the subject        {"type":"bigtext","text":"1925","slot":"back","color":"accent2"}
  asset        halftone cut-out, `ground` shadow            {"type":"asset","name":"ct_x","slot":"center","halftone":"bw","spot":"accent2"}
  photo        torn halftone print                          {"type":"photo","src":"sc_x","frame":"torn","slot":"left"}
  block        colour slab / circle / glitch edge           {"type":"block","shape":"circle","color":"accent2","slot":"back"}
  note         thin paper annotation label                  {"type":"note","text":"...","slot":"l1"}
  title        `box:"strip"` highlighter strip + `sub`      {"type":"title","text":"...","sub":"..."}
  arrow/loops  hand-drawn dashed arrow / pen loops          {"type":"arrow","from":"a","to":"b"}
  badge        UI chip (play/star/hash/check/bell/mail...)  {"type":"badge","icon":"play","slot":"l1"}
  ransom       ransom-note letters                          {"type":"ransom","text":"WHY","slot":"l1"}
  chart        textured bar chart                           {"type":"chart","rows":[{"label":"..","value":42}],"slot":"band"}
  counter, list, timeline, callout, stamp, lower_third also work

LAYOUTS (names usable in EDIT.json)
  numeral_collage  giant numeral behind a centre cut-out, notes either side, small cut-outs low-left/right
  strip_hero       strip title + sub-line, ONE big cut-out, notes either side
  desk_collage     title strip, five cut-outs scattered around a circle/block, arrows + loops between them
  chart_grid       serif title with sub-line, bar chart band
  framed_portrait  gilt-framed portrait with a name/role lower-third (use bg "chalk")
  wide_cutout      wide halftone building/panorama with two colour blocks and a title top-left

AVOID  dark cinematic grading, letterbox, neon, sticker outlines, vector pills, more than one spot colour,
       colour photos shown un-halftoned next to halftone ones, centred layout for every scene
```

## Stickman Storytelling  (`stickman`)

```text
Stickman Storytelling: an expressive comic stickman explainer with selective colour.

IDENTITY
  A funny, sharp storyteller. A simple stick figure with a clean white round head acts out the idea in hand-drawn
  scenes: reacting, pointing, falling, climbing, arguing with itself. The comedy is in the POSE and the FACE, the
  clarity is in the staging. It reads like a modern comic strip that moves.

CANVAS
  Warm paper #F5F1E6 for diagram beats, or a full hand-drawn 2D scene (cliff, desk, road, cave, city) drawn in muted
  slate / ink cross-hatching so the coloured hero pops. Never a photograph.

PALETTE (selective colour)
  Scenes stay muted (slate, warm grey, paper). Only the stickman's outfit and the ONE hero prop carry saturated colour:
  cobalt #2A6BD1, marker red #D63C28 or warm amber #F0AF0F. One saturated colour per scene, plus ink black and white.

CHARACTER
  White round head, black ink contour 3 to 5 px, dot or oval eyes, expressive eyebrows, simple mouth (smirk, shock,
  grin, frown). Limbs are clean lines with readable hands. Outfit comes from the story: the cast in SHOTS.json
  `world.characters`, or clothing that fits the era and job (caveman tunic, Roman soldier, office shirt, lab coat).
  Same face, line weight and proportions in every shot.

TYPOGRAPHY
  Handwritten Kalam for titles (2 to 5 words). Labels 1 to 3 words in plain dark ink under the drawing.
  Emphasis: doodle circle, marker underline, one sticky note. At most ONE speech bubble per scene, 1 to 4 words.

COMPOSITION
  Rule of thirds: the character on one third, the thing it reacts to on the other. Big empty paper for text.
  Rows of 2 to 4 labelled drawings for lists and comparisons.

MOTION
  Snappy but clean: pop with settle, slide in, quick whip transitions between story beats, gentle drift on scenes.
  No continuous wobble or shaking.

SIGNATURE MOVES
  whiteboard hero (character + circled keyword) · reaction beat (character + big prop) · labelled row · napkin math
  (two sides of a comparison) · pop title for questions and punchlines

AVOID
  photoreal people or places, 3D renders, glossy gradients, more than one saturated colour per scene, speech-bubble
  clutter, the same outfit for every era, paragraphs of text.
```

```text
Sticker Hybrid: pop-art vinyl die-cut stickers on tactile paper.

IDENTITY

  shadow) slapped onto crumpled paper or a cutting mat. Satire through visual metaphor: a brain handing out advice,
  a parody product, a burning wallet. Punchy and clean, never cluttered.

CANVAS
  Crumpled off-white paper #F4F1EA (default), graph grid for data beats, green cutting mat for "desk" flat-lays.

PALETTE
  ink #141416 · crimson #E63946 (problem, punchline) · sunflower #FFB703 (idea, money, highlight) ·
  mint #06D6A0 (good, solution). The stickers themselves are vivid; the page stays neutral. One accent leads per scene.

TYPOGRAPHY
  Rounded heavy display (Baloo 2 ExtraBold) for titles, 2 to 5 words. Labels as small pills under stickers.

COMPOSITION

MOTION
  Slap-on pop with a gentle settle, quick whip transitions, soft drift. No shaking, no vibrating stickers.
```

## Suspense Noir / Crime Thriller  (`noir`)

```text
Suspense Noir / Crime Thriller — true-crime noir engine.

IDENTITY
  A prestige true-crime documentary. Full-bleed cinematic chiaroscuro photography carries most beats; the case
  is assembled on top of it with case-file tags, redaction bars that slide away on the reveal word, polaroids on
  a red-string board and forensic cut-outs. Slow, tense, investigative; every frame feels like evidence.

The visual language of prestige true-crime documentary series and investigative podcasts turned into video:
cold opens, case files, deep noir photography, red-string evidence boards,
polaroid portraits pinned to cork, redaction bars that slide to reveal, CCTV framing with
timestamps, forensic callouts tagged like exhibits, cinema letterbox and 35mm grain.

WHAT MAKES IT WORK
  * cinema-wide letterbox (7.5%) so every frame reads as "footage, not image"
  * cold cyan shadows + warm amber highlights, with blood-red used ONLY on stamps/strings
  * never leave the frame bare -- a photo, a pin, a label, a timestamp, something
  * redactions and reveals are the signature move: hide a name until the beat it drops
  * the rubber stamp SLAMS with screen shake and sub-bass -- but only TWICE per video (the reveal, the verdict);
    other emphasis beats are tabs, tape, seals, scrawls, redactions (core/engine/variety.py enforces it)
  * every photo is a polaroid with hand-written caption and a red/amber pushpin
  * evidence boards (`board` layout) connect photos with red string on named cues
  * CCTV-framed photos get a timestamp overlay like "03:14:22 // CAM 04"

ESSENTIAL ELEMENTS
  kicker        case-file tag: "CASE FILE #4092-A // EVIDENCE 03"
  title         bold claim with one *word* in blood-red
  redact        black bar that slides away on a named cue (prime suspect reveal)
  stamp         CLASSIFIED / COLD CASE / PRIME SUSPECT -- design rubber (max 2) | tab | tape | seal | scrawl
  photo         polaroid frame with handwritten caption (default)
  callout       forensic tag "EXHIBIT A -- 9mm shell" pointing to a detail
  string        red yarn connecting two board items (evidence connections)
  lower_third   witness statement with role under the name
  timeline      case chronology with timestamps

LAYOUTS unique to this style
  evidence_board     cork-pin photos + central stamp + strings between slots
  interrogation      centred face, left/right call-outs
  cctv_grid          four CCTV photo panels with timestamps
  dossier            open folder spread: documents left, photos right
  cold_open          giant redacted title + case kicker + ticking clock

AVOID
  * stickers, doodle accents, bright primary colours, speech bubbles
  * clean/soft caption plates (use `cinema` with amber highlight)
  * fast whip transitions (dip-to-black and slow fades sell the genre)

IMAGE PROMPTS come out as low-key chiaroscuro cinema photography:
hard practical light sources (single desk lamp, interrogation bulb, headlights, torch),
desaturated teal shadows, one warm amber highlight, deep crushed shadows, volumetric haze,
heavy 35mm grain, matte-painting detail.
```

## Hand-Drawn Doodle  (`doodle`)

```text
Hand-Drawn Doodle: a warm sketchbook explainer drawn in crayon and felt-tip ink.

IDENTITY
  A smart friend explains the idea by drawing it on good paper while talking. Every frame is one clear
  visual metaphor drawn with thick, slightly imperfect black crayon lines, and coloured with only three
  crayons. It feels handmade, warm and calm. Nothing is photographic and nothing is glossy.

CANVAS
  Warm off-white fine-tooth paper #F8F6EF, always. No pure #FFFFFF, no dark pages, no gradients, no vignette.
  Occasional notebook margin rule (the `margin_notes` layout). Bottom 15% of every frame stays EMPTY: captions live there.

PALETTE (strict 3-crayon rule)
  ink black for every line · sunflower yellow · cobalt blue · tomato red.
  One crayon colour carries the meaning in each scene (yellow = idea/insight, blue = fact/calm, red = problem/warning).
```

## Retro 8-Bit Arcade  (`arcade`)

```text
Retro 8-Bit Arcade: the story told as a 16-bit console game.

IDENTITY
  The topic becomes a video game: chapters are STAGES, people are SPRITES, problems are BOSSES, numbers are SCORES
  and HP bars, decisions are dialogue boxes. Everything is authentic 16-bit era pixel art (the console generation
  after 8-bit): a visible pixel grid, a limited palette, dithered shading. The name says "8-bit" because that is
  what viewers call the look; the art standard is 16-bit so it stays rich and readable.

CANVAS
  Deep navy night #10101C behind full-frame pixel stages (side-scrolling levels, top-down maps, boss arenas).
  CRT finishing: soft scanlines and a slight vignette (the renderer's grade + vignette).

PALETTE (limited, like a real console)
  navy #10101C base · arcade gold #FFD700 (score, coins, titles) · retro cyan #00E1FF (UI, player) ·
  arcade crimson #FF325F (danger, boss, damage). At most these three accents plus the sprites' own palette.

TYPOGRAPHY
  Monospaced bold (Space Mono) in UPPERCASE for HUD text: "STAGE 2", "HP", "SCORE 35000", "GAME OVER".

IMAGE LANGUAGE (what Flow must draw)
  16-bit pixel art with a consistent pixel size, hard pixel edges, no anti-aliasing, dithered shadows.

COMPOSITION
  HUD discipline: title / stage name top-left, score or counter top-right, the sprite in the middle third,

MOTION
```
